A label is the name your organisation writes for a period, such as `FY2023 Q4`. The `naming` block of a definition holds one or more schemes, and each scheme has a template for some of the levels `year`, `quarter`, `period` and `week` ([spec](/reference/spec/definition-v1/#naming)).

## Templates

A template is literal text with placeholders. `{yyyy}` is the year number, `{yy}` its last two digits, `{yy_next}` the last two digits of the next year number, `{q}` the quarter, `{pp}` the period and `{ww}` the week. Every template must contain `{yyyy}` or `{yy}`, and a quarter, period or week template must contain its own index, so that every label names exactly one period of one year. The [templates table](/reference/spec/definition-v1/#templates) in the spec gives the padding of each placeholder and the levels it is allowed in.

A template that uses `{yy}` without `{yyyy}` needs a `century_pivot`, which says which century a two-digit year belongs to when a label is parsed.

## Format and parse

`parse` matches the whole label, case-sensitively, against every template of every scheme. A label that matches nothing returns `LABEL_NOT_RECOGNISED`.

## Ambiguous labels

When a label matches more than one period, `parse` does not pick one. It returns `AMBIGUOUS_LABEL` with every candidate in the error's `details`, ordered by start date, then scheme name, then level ([spec](/reference/spec/api-v1/#parse)).

This happens, for example, when two schemes use the same template but number years differently. `FY2024` is the year that starts on 4 Feb 2024 under `by_start`, and the year that ends on 3 Feb 2024 under `by_end`. Each candidate's `year` is its number under the default scheme, `by_start`, so the `by_end` reading appears as year 2023. To make every label unambiguous, give each scheme a template that the others cannot match, such as `FY{yyyy}` and `FYE{yyyy}`.
