fiscalclock has no built-in calendars. You describe your calendar as a JSON definition, and every answer comes from that definition.

## Write a definition

Save this definition as `retail-4-5-4.json`. It describes a 4-5-4 retail calendar: each fiscal year ends on the Saturday nearest 31 January, each quarter has periods of 4, 5 and 4 weeks, a 53-week year puts its extra week at the end of period 12, and labels look like `FY2023 Q4`.

<Code code={retail454Json} lang="json" title="retail-4-5-4.json" />

This is an example, not a published calendar of any organisation. It is the definition of the conformance vector [`nrf-53-week-years.json`](https://github.com/Karthik-beta/fiscalclock/blob/main/conformance/vectors/cited/nrf-53-week-years.json), whose year rule gives the 53-week years that the [NRF publishes for its 4-5-4 calendar](https://nrf.com/resources/4-5-4-calendar). The period that takes the extra week and the label templates are this example's own choices.

[Write a definition](/guides/write-a-definition/) explains each block.

3 Feb 2024 is the last day of fiscal year 2023, in its fourth quarter and in week 53, the extra week. Fiscal year 2023 starts on 29 Jan 2023 and ends before 4 Feb 2024: every period is a half-open interval with an exclusive end, as [Dates and intervals](/guides/dates-and-intervals/) explains.
